<h1 align="center">✧ You don't understand NTM ✧</h1>

<sub><b>Data:</b> 27/05/2018 | <b>Section:</b> Banter </sub>

`@batzion:`    

Alex Jones. David Duke. Terence McKenna. NobodyTM. 

What do these names have in common? You find internet dwellers posting them on political imageboards. That’s it - at least for the purposes of our critique. Nine opens his piece by casually lumping NobodyTM (NTM) alongside ideologues whose speeches leave zero doubt about what you’re supposed to think by the time the audio cuts out. NTM does not follow that pattern. In reality, that clumsy opening grouping tells you everything you need to know about Nine, and precisely nothing about NTM. When an NTM video ends, Nine expects a pre-chewn conclusion. Upon discovering that he has instead been left alone in an uncomfortable vacuum of ambiguity, he panics and instantly fills the void with his own pre-packaged forum dogma—acting exactly the way he would when digesting any other partisan slop from his favorite BBS echo chambers.

Is this a valid, intelligent reading of NTM? Absolutely not—unless, like Nine, you are physically incapable of viewing human culture through any lens other than a strict left-right culture-war prism.

Further down, Nine acknowledges two crucial points: first, that NTM has no identifiable "self" outside the work; and second, that the art's message is inherently subjective. Yet, despite explicitly admitting this, Nine immediately turns around and slaps lazy, reductive labels on NTM anyway, calling them "priestly," "antimodern," and "anti-hedonistic." Those labels are purely for the author to fill in the gaps, and Nine assumes NTM must share these goals because the other media he consumes on political message boards preaches that exact gospel. Nine describes how NTM's work "force-fed [him] a combination of honey and krokodil before thrusting him into the deathly cold of space," yet this grand spiritual transformation apparently wasn't strong enough to loosen Nine's grip on his own rigid left-right political sensibilities. NTM recontextualises the rotten, the absurd, and the beautiful to cast them in a new light, but Nine lacks either the charity or the artistic literacy to view NTM outside the narrow forum threads where he discovered it.

The main work that the author describes is "You". The piece opens with imagery of young dancers in a studio twerking and moving suggestively on a distorted screen, interlaced with hazy, dreamlike colours. The accompanying audio features aspeech about the strategic deployment of pornography as a psychological warfare tactic to pacify populations in Ramallah. How does Nine describe this?

> "It graphically pushes before us things which might make our skin crawl or our brows furrow; there isn’t much use of words – even the words spoken serve a secondary role – as the visual and aural set the tone."   

To claim there "isn't much use of words" is to miss the entire mechanics of the piece, seeing as the audio track is exactly what transforms the video from an uncut clip of women dancing to a work of juxtaposition. Juxtaposition cannot exist in a vacuum. 

Nine isolates the visual element because his forum-conditioned brain has a immediate, knee-jerk reaction: women dancing in short clothes = moral decay = gross. Because the spoken track doesn't hand him a neatly packaged conclusion, he dismisses it as secondary background flavour. Nine knows what he’s supposed to think about "slutty" women, but he doesn't know how to process that imagery when juxtaposed with systemic control and warfare, because that level of nuance isn't discussed on political BBS. He fails to see the whole picture, and that's a critical failure when the main tool used is one that requires you to see all the details *together*. 

It becomes glaringly obvious that Nine is entirely unfamiliar with this lineage of avant-garde media art. He openly admits that he first encountered NTM on imageboards where works were shared by fellow political edge-lords. NTM occupies a unique space—I cannot think of a single art collective that managed to get so thoroughly "hugged" and misinterpreted by those specific internet subcultures. It's clear when Nine points out that NTM calls how it mixes its media 'editing', which is how everyone would call it, not just NTM. Granted, one does not need to know the terminology to be familiar with something, but I couldn't help but think of that scene in Inglorious Basterds where the spy gets the ordering signal wrong - Nine failed to pronounce the shibboleth by assuming that term was something special to NTM. 

This lack of familiarity and art-literacy is even more clear when you read the author's points about the special episodes, such as "UFO ZONE". Nine says there's a 'gotcha' as the crux to those episodes. But what is it? He doesn't specify. 

In reality, there is no gotcha - NTM would make those special episodes with a mixture of sarcasm and genuine fascination. There was no critique against the points made towards the people who believe UFOs are real and there's chemtrails in the sky. It was presented with effort to make it look cohesive, even changing the usual aesthetics of NTM to match the topic. It wasn't ridiculing them, it simply assumed that the viewer would understand that it IS ridiculous. It operates in the same spirit as those classic South Park episodes on Scientology or Mormonism—where simply animating a group's literal beliefs line-for-line acts as the ultimate satire because the premises are inherently ridiculous. Nine fails to realize that by framing right-wing conspiracies as a freak show, NTM is holding up a mirror to the viewer. The real question NTM poses isn't "Isn't modernity evil?" but rather: "Why do you readily accept the critique in 'You,' but try to distance yourself from the absurdities presented in 'UFO ZONE'?" Unlike Alex Jones, who preaches conspiracies with screaming, calculated sincerity so you'll buy his survival slop, NTM lays them out as part of the broader, tragicomic circus of human coping mechanisms.

Nine asserts:

> "The artwork of NTM is clearly modern in form. The reuse and repurposing of content [...] makes up the bulk of what exists on nobodytm.com. The using of modern forms, of contemporary styles, to create a fundamentally antimodern message is a trope which does exist elsewhere – and is the general focus of this piece of writing." 

Huh. NTM uses modern digital tools, relies on hyper-contemporary aesthetics, and draws heavily from internet subcultures like vaporwave rather than ancient, traditionalist iconography. NTM understands modernity intimately—its contributors would wade through hundreds of hours of modern digital sludge to assemble their tapestries. Of course NTM contains critiques of modern life—even love songs contain subtle critiques of human nature if you have functional ears to listen. But Nine slaps the label "antimodern" across his entire piece, even the title, because he needs them to fit neatly into his predetermined box. 

I disagree entirely. I don't find NTM to be anti-anything. The art simply acknowledges that contemporary human life contains the rotten, the transcendental, the vulgar, and the sublime all at once—and that all of these elements exist simultaneously without canceling each other out. Choosing a rigid ideological side in a culture war is entirely pointless, because regardless of which tribe you pledge allegiance to, the rest of human reality remains stubbornly intact.

To label NTM "antimodern" misses the core function of the project: culture jamming. Bricolage. You can critique elements of a civilisation from within it, but being flatly anti-that-civilisation is a completely different theological can of worms that I am convinced NTM never opened. It is clear that NTM understands posturing. If they were to anchor their artworks firmly in one political camp or the other, it would instantly suffocate their reach and turn the art into dead propaganda. The medium itself tells you everything you need to know: NTM uses the very digital sludge they are critiquing to forge art, breathing brand-new life into old data streams. How can you wage a holy war against your own vehicle?

There is no urgent call to action in NTM’s work telling you to "fight modernity" or "return to traditional agrarianism." When they flash "Beware the God of the Machines," remember that the message is coming straight from the machine itself. We acknowledge the tools handed to us by all ideological camps, we expose ourselves to the digital radiation, and therefore we must forge new meaning out of the wreckage. Your own political camp is just as disgusting, vain, and ridiculous when placed under the correct analytical light, and that's irrelevant to the fact that you may be a oversexualised furry leftist or right-ist that thinks chemicals in the water turns frogs gay. We are all deeply ridiculous. We are all living in a decaying, mediated world. And we make art out of the scraps. That is where actual transcendence occurs.

Nine writes:

"Nearly every scene is a shock-scare, to use one phrase. Not that they are animalistically frightening, but these often strange and niche displays of various types are forced into an area where they are not desired for the original intention if at all. Values are inverted in more ways than one. Safe to say, much of NTM’s video content is not ordinary by any means." 

Is it genuinely designed to "shock" you? Or do you only assume it's shocking because of where you happened to stumble across the media?

To an isolated right-wing imageboard dweller, seeing women dancing in skimpy clothes is treated like a profound moral shock. But did Nine ever stop to consider that NTM never posted their works to those BBS boards in the first place? The tinted, hostile light through which he views the art is entirely a product of his own environment. Did he ever stop to consider that, aside from the thematic juxtaposition, NTM may have chosen beautiful, scantily clad women dancing suggestively precisely so the intended viewer would find it smooth and hypnotic compared to the heavy, disturbing geopolitical audio—which Nine couldn't even be bothered to properly analyse in his text?

Nine and his progressive liberal counterpart are far more similar than either of them would ever care to admit. They both demand that art function as a moral billboard. He talked about his friend's point as a juxtaposition to his own "naked mind", yet he completely failed to realise that his reaction was just more of the same predictable tribal posturing, just packaged in a different flavour of retardation and stationed on a different coordinate of the political scale.

Ultimately, Nine displays a profound psychological inability to process anything that threatens his rigid worldview, and that intellectual cowardice is what locked him into viewing NTM solely through the flat, gray walls of an imageboard thread.

NTM is art. Not a political campaign post.
